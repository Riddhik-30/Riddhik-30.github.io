Project Reflection

From an idea to a community.

In 2025, I founded RiznikMUN, a free, student-led online Model United Nations initiative. What began as an idea became a two-day conference with 50 participants and a 15-person team working across delegate affairs, outreach, marketing, sponsorship communication, and event operations. I was responsible not only for creating the initiative but also for coordinating people with different responsibilities and keeping the event moving toward a common goal. This experience taught me that leadership is not simply about having an idea or being the person in charge. I learned that successful leadership requires delegation, communication, accountability, and the ability to adapt when things do not go according to plan. I also realized that creating opportunities for others can be just as meaningful as achieving something individually. Seeing participants engage with an event that my team and I had built made me more confident in my ability to turn an idea into something tangible. As I begin my journey at UC, I want to apply these lessons to team projects, student organizations, and eventually professional environments. As a computer science student, I know that technical skills alone are not enough; solving problems often requires communicating ideas and working effectively with others. This experience shaped my definition of a Global Citizen Scholar as someone who takes initiative, learns from different perspectives, and creates opportunities for others while contributing to a larger community.
